PERSONAL REFLECTIONS / JOURNAL

Evan Journal.

Notes on consciousness, freedom, discipline, recognition, and the making of a self.

Define consciousness and how your understanding has changed: I used to understand consciousness as the thing that determined how I live. I thought consciousness was us living in the moment, making our own decisions for ourselves, and being able to determine right from wrong. I also thought that we had a free consciousness and that we could do whatever we wanted to. Define freedom: For freedom, I understand freedom as the ability to do whatever you want, whenever you want. People get jobs so that they can get money so that they can achieve freedom. When did you use intentional repetition/discipline in your life One of the things that I had to use repetition and intentional behavior in was piano. I was preparing a very difficult piece called La Campanella and it took me nearly 2 years of practicing almost every day to achieve. There were many times where I wanted to give up and stop playing and do something else but I was able to perform the piece to a pretty high standard at the end of it all. It taught me to look at life from a more long-term standpoint and also that frustration is all a part of the journey. I think I have become a much more patient and understanding person because of that. Which definition of freedom relates more to you? I think for me discipline relates much more than detachment. Like Maeve, I think a lot of the choices I make are based off what I am attached to, not what i am detached from. A lot of my life also revolve around discipline, such as practicing the piano, doing schoolwork even when I don’t want to, and going to the gym. Because of this, I would have to reject the detachment model and I would say that my experience is much more applicable to the discipline model. Reflection on lecture: I think the difference between discipline and detachment is very interesting. One one hand, you have to work very hard to attain consciousness and it takes a lot of discipline and suffering to truly become free. But on the other hand, people like Jaynes argue that development of consciousness is about stepping back and evaluating your thoughts and emotions before you act. I was a little confused about the detachment part of lecture so I’m not exactly sure if what I’m saying is right or not. What do you understand differently about your reading that you did not understand before: The thing I understand differently about the master-slave relationship from today’s lecture is that I think the idea of discipline can be applied to this. Before I used to think that the slave was doing work purely out of fear of the master. However, I think the slave uses a lot of discipline when he is putting in work to try to free himself from the master. This is probably not work that he really wants to be doing but he does it anyway because it is the only way to be free. So after all that suffering and work he is able to look back and his consciousness has now developed further and he sees himself as a true conscious being. What is a connection between Hegel and Scarry: Both Scarry and Hegel talk about a shared focus on the relationship between consciousness and the external world. Hegel argues that consciousness develops into self-consciousness through one’s experiences with the outside world. Individuals come to understand themselves through their relationships with the world and other people. Scarry focuses on how physical pain is used to create objects. Hegel describes action and creation as a way for a person to externalize themselves and recognize themselves in the world, but Scarry argues that severe pain can make this externalization difficult because pain is internal and hard to communicate if you are not experiencing it. Both writers are therefore concerned with how inner experience becomes connected to the outside world, but they emphasize different possibilities. What confused you about the Scarry reading today: One thing that really confused me about Scarry was the reciprocity of the object. She says that when we make/put an action onto an object, we are giving the object some of our characteristics (like our life being vulnerable and not immortal) but then we get back some of that immortality through reciprocity which doesn’t make sense to me. Summary of Scarry: Scarry uses the idea of physical pain to prove that our experiences and perception and feeling of “what it is to be me” is not reducible to brain activity. She does this by working through a five step process: pain, projection, animation, identification, and empathy. She argues that we need to treat objects as people before we can treat people as people. What quality of consciousness did you choose: The thing that I chose to write about was phenomenality. I think this is easy to write about because it is the easiest to understand out of the 5 in my opinion, as it is simply “what it feels like to be me” . I also feel like it is pretty easy to talk about in the context of Hegel. However, I am struggling with fully understanding the actual reading and that is making the writing of it pretty difficult. How Wittgenstein may describe consciousness: I think Wittgenstein would describe consciousness as something that probably cannot be defined. It has a complex network of traits and characteristics but other animals also have the same traits and so there is not one defining trait of consciousness that differentiates it from something else. While our common sense may say that consciousness has one defining trait, this may not be the case when you actually look at it. For example, if we think about reflexivity vs phenominality, there is a lot of difference in those two traits and both can maybe be replicated by animals. But it is hard to connect each concept of consciousness to the next. Some people’s definitions may overlap and some may have nothing to do with each other and both can be valid. What Wittgenstein would say about freedom: Similar to consciousness, I think Wittgenstein would say that there is no easy way to define freedom. Many different philosophers have different definitions and it is more like a network than a simple term. This means that some people may agree with others and some people may not agree with those same people and both can have a valid argument. Freedom is also used in various contexts. For example, the freedom to make your own decisions is different than being physically free and not held captive, so they may have more/less objectivity depending on the situation/context. What Wittgenstein would say about “self”: Again, like the previous two, Wittgenstein would probably say that the self is not defined by one characteristic, but the overlap of many different characteristics. Many people may have different ideas on what the self actually is and how they are different from others. But there may also be some people that share qualities with other people and some that share none. Therefore, this system of overlapping/not is how we can determine our self and individuality. It will be a longer process which is fine but we definitely need to figure it out soon. How our individual topic applies to the audience’s cognitive thinking throughout watching Westworld: Hegel’s ideas about continuity and reconsolidation shape how the audience understands identity throughout Westworld. Hegel argues that consciousness must reconsider its sense of self when what once made that identity feel stable begins to fall apart. The hosts experience this as their memories challenge the realities they have accepted and force them to reconsider who they are. Similarly, the audience must revise its understanding of the hosts as new information changes the meaning of their past experiences. This connects to Hegel’s argument that identity is not simply preserved but must be reconstructed as consciousness confronts contradictions in its existing understanding of itself. As viewers discover that the hosts’ memories and identities are more complicated than they initially appeared, they must decide how to interpret what they previously believed. In this way, Westworld encourages the audience to experience a version of reconsolidation themselves, questioning whether a stable sense of identity comes from holding onto the past or reconsidering what that past means. What is an example of an object that you have transferred some of your emotions into: When I used to play piano, I was practicing a really difficult piece and it was very frustrating because some days I would play it really good and other days I would not. I started to notice that when I was frustrated I would unconsciously play louder and faster as if I was trying to prove that I could play it as good as the day before. What is something that resonated with you in lecture today: I think the idea between fatalism and freedom is something that was really interesting to me and something that I didn’t think about when I first read paradise lost. Satan is stuck on this idea of himself, and he thinks that he possesses this one true self and there is no way for him to change that. His selfhood exists entirely by identifying himself with another. Whereas for Eve, freedom is the ability to be able to change your idea about yourself and understand that there may not be one true self. I think there is a spectrum and most people fall somewhere in the middle, where they know they have values that they are not willing to change but there are many things about their personality that can change over time. Identify some internal conflict you have experienced and try mapping it onto an internal master-slave dialectic When I played piano, I used to be able to read the sheet music and I never really had to study for my lessons. But then, the music got a lot more technically difficult, and I had a few bad lessons so I had to start playing a lot in order to achieve my goal of being able to play these really difficult pieces up on stage in front of a lot of people. It was very hard, and it required desire held in check as Hegel said because there were many times where I really did not want to do the work, but at the end the work paid off and I was able to perform all the pieces that I wanted to. Identify one sustained undertaking in your life that ended up changing your view of who you were. One thing that I started to undertake in my life was I refused to get anything below an A in high school. If I had an A-, I would have anxiety about it until my grade was resolved. It really pissed me off whenever I had a missing assignment or I didn’t do as well on a test as I should have. When I look back on my high school years, I realize how that mindset has built the foundation of myself today. I no longer look at myself as needing to be perfect, but I understand that if you strive for perfection and you don’t reach it, you will still end up in a good spot. ​
