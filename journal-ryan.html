Notes on consciousness, freedom, discipline, recognition, and the making of a self.

Honors 241 Personal Journal 9/9/26 Disc. How I Used to Understand Consciousness: I used to think consciousness was simply being aware of yourself and your surroundings. I didn’t think there was any conflict between consciousness and its surroundings or other external factors. Just a mind that knows it is a knowing entity. I understand freedom in the sense that while our own biology and other factors encourage us to make certain decisions, it is ultimately our own conscious choice to choose to commit certain acts, think certain thoughts, and believe in certain things. 9/16/26 Disc. An experience I can relate to reading is always questioning things in school and whether or not the system itself is trying to make me perceive something a certain way, like in history class, for example. This parallels the evil demon concept from Descartes. Something I would like to understand more from the lecture is maybe how Descartes interprets mathematical concepts as being real, and not just something an evil demon put into his head. 9/23/26 Lect. 1: Only once I started hitting the gym consistently did I start to better understand how hard work pays off over time, little by little. It gave me a new appreciation for my own physical health and got me addicted to achieving hard goals. I learned a lot about my body, nutrition, and the movements. Before this, I wasn’t truly concerned with any of this and was significantly more lazy and idle. The gym made me happier, more confident, and altered me in many ways. I even made friends through this hobby, and it altered my path in life. 2: I think discipline relates much more to my experience, mainly because that is the pathway in which many of my habits, thoughts, and ideas have developed. I am still rooted in reality, but when faced with options, instead of detaching, I choose a more predisposed path. I stick more to rules and regimen. I have a lot of habits, and my behavior is somewhat fixed, in my opinion. 9/23/26 Disc. Reflecting on the lecture, I thought about the entire phenomenon of how even the choice to step back and question reality and free will may even be programmed into certain people. What I mean is, I thought it was interesting how this, in itself, may not be a real choice but rather something that we may think is a choice or an enlightened decision, but is, in fact, the opposite. In subsection 190 of the Hegel reading, something I kinda understand differently now is that the idea that the master servant relationship ends with the servant achieving a more independent and enlightened sense of self may be somewhat flawed in reasoning when combined with other ideas of consciousness. This sense of independence may not be a response to the dynamic but could simply be predetermined in the brain, as was discussed in lecture. 9/28/26 Disc. I think Scarry connects to Hegel by describing how humans have needs, and through those needs, they create things which then exceed their expectations and help them develop. Scarry describes this using objects, while Hegel seems to do this using the master servant idea. Humans have apparent desires, and these desires result in a conflict; the result of that conflict, while providing an expected outcome, also helps them- more specifically, the servant- to develop further in unexpected ways. 9/30/26 Lect. Scarry talks about how people, in order to truly empathize with other individuals, have to first empathize with inanimate objects. Among these ideas, there are stages in which this happens. Pain, projection, animation, identification, and empathization. First, you feel an indescribable pain, which you then project onto something else. You then find comfort in that thing, start to identify with it, and then finally you feel empathy for it. 9/30/26 Disc. For build 3, I chose reflexivity because Hegel talks a lot about how people’s consciousnesses develop through the interactions between each other. Specifically, Hegel talks about a master servant diuretic, and a battle between them that gives them their roles. In the battle, one person has to be completely willing to die; that person usually wins. Reflexivity is the quality of when a person recognizes their consciousness is in a different state than the usual in one's environment, and I think this is achieved during this battle, and in the servant afterward. I did not really have any questions or have trouble doing this. 10/7/26 Lect. I think Wittgenstein would describe consciousness as a collective knowledge of different things rather than just one thing. It's a combination of a plethora of knowledge and connections. I think Wittgenstein would define freedom as an illusion. One might believe they have the freedom to make decisions and even be aware of their choices, but in reality, this is false. Social norms largely influence and determine the choices of people. Wittgenstein would say that the sense of self is an overlap of many different qualities, as well as a culmination of a majority of thoughts, ideas, and relationships. 10/7/26 Disc. Our website does not work. Our agent has yet to perform any of our demands. 10/12/26 Disc. My writing as of now has explored the relationship between reflexivity and Hegelian ideas. In Westworld, the hosts become somewhat reflexive, understanding that they are entities with awareness; however, the level of awareness is quite ambiguous. Towards the end, it's clear that Maeve and Dolores reach this reflexivity, but along the way, it's unclear. Maeve thinks she is fully conscious only to realize that something is giving her thoughts milliseconds before she “comes up” with them. This follows reflexivity being a spectrum in that she is partially reflexive but not entirely autonomous. 10/28/26 Disc. In lecture today, I thought it was very interesting when Professor Hoffman discussed the different levels of free choice. It really resonated with me- the 4 levels- and, related to those, having made a choice on every level. I never truly understood this in an organized way until today, when he explained it. 11/4/26 Lect. Internally, I have been struggling with wanting to work extremely hard, to put progress over experience, and with wanting to experience life to the fullest without worrying about the “rat race.” This has been extremely hard. Sometimes I want to go hang out with friends, go to parties, travel, all these things. Sometimes I want to say, “away with this,” and go extremely hard at academics, the gym, career aspirations, things like this. I believe I am the slave to the external world in this way. I’m letting all these things control how I feel and how I think about myself. However, I know that if I stop letting these things control me, I would be able to create a better balance. Instead of trying to live up to a label like “hard worker,” I would feel happier and fulfilled. 11/9/26 Lect. One sustained undertaking was probably high school. Starting out, I didn’t view myself as very smart or talented, but as I continued through high school and took on new challenges, I discovered that I have a lot of talent and am a lot smarter than I originally thought. I have a lot more to offer and am more capable than when I first began.
