Elisa Journal.

Notes on consciousness, freedom, discipline, recognition, and the making of a self.

Personal journal How I used to understand consciousness I used to think of consciousness as something people possessed and utilized at different capacities depending on the situation. Being aware, in any sense of the word, of one’s existence in the world. How I understand freedom I think of freedom as the ultimate goal for a semi-conscious being. Being free is to rid oneself of a prior obstacle or limitation. Write about lecture/reading → personal response Section of team reading that i need to dig deeper into I really enjoyed Descartes’s meditations as I resonated with his ideas on how consciousness is free and accidental to some extent. His thought process of eliminating previous beliefs in order to construct a fully acceptable response to “what is consciousness” was very interesting. It made me realize that while encounters with mistakes and falsehoods are inevitable, it is only through breaking down the very foundation of our understanding of the mind, that one can reach their personal truths regarding consciousness. Coming into the reading, I wasn't sure what dualism was as a concept. When I was attributed the identity of a “dualist” by professor Hoffman, I was indeed confused. It was uncomfortable to be labeled as something I did not understand yet. I don’t think we’re the same person throughout our whole existence. If we live in a world that is constantly changing, it would be illogical to think that we don’t change along with it. People will never treat “ In that experience it has been quite undone, has trembled in every fiber of its being, and everything solid and stable has been shaken to its foundations. Yet, this pure universal movement-the absolute melting away of everything stable-proves to be the simple, essential nature of self-consciousness itself, absolute negativity, pure being-for-self [...]” I initially had a hard time differentiating consciousness from the self, and from the mind. I used to use those words interchangeably, thus attributing the development of a self and of a mind to the provocation of fear and acts of labor from the slave. I found Hegel incredibly confusing during the first reading, and interpreted his argument as “the fear of death overtakes the slave’s entire being, this fear now establishes his new sense of self”. Now I see it more as a catalytic factor that led to the slave’s initial stage of consciousness, allowing to focus his entire existence on the premise of existing FOR the self. This is rather different from attributing any consciousness to a sense of self, which is what I previously held to believe. I now know that individuality, identity, sense of self, and soul are all different from each other. I also used to believe that the repetition of labor wasn’t something relevant to the development of consciousness, but now know the studied effects of automation and practice in developing autonomy and power, enhancing our impressions of intentionality. How Scarry can go in conversation with Hegel Scarry’s idea of projecting pain through creation is an extension of Hegel’s argument regarding work and creation. Hegel attributes the slave’s consciousness to his ability to create something tangible through his work. The materialization of his labor gives his existence purpose, and the intentionality behind his work is a testament to his “being for the self”. Scarry explores this idea with a new depth. She highlights the projection of sentience onto the objects of our creation as a way to communicate our own suffering. As a result, the inanimate and unfeeling retain a part of our sentience and develop a consciousness of their own. Scarry argues that the main reason we project our sentience onto objects is because we want to outwardly show our aversiveness to sentience in unfeeling subjects. We create and imagine things in order to accommodate ourselves, and it all starts from the concept of pain. For my build 3, I’ve chosen the concept of reflexivity – the awareness that one is aware of something, different from normal awareness of one's environment. Generally, this occurs during moments of stress, crisis, or perceived threat – anytime in which a non-impulsive decision needs to be reached. I chose this concept because it was the one that appealed most to me when thinking about Hegel’s ideas and seeing Professor Hoffman’s comments on our previous builds. I used it to distinguish consciousness from self-consciousness and how that applied to interdependence between people. I wanted to explore how this awareness was in fact a new layer of consciousness that needs to be worked on to achieve, which I thought applied very well to the concept of labor in the master-slave dialectic. I also contemplated relating Hegel to Scarry’s idea of projection, but while somewhat relevant, I thought it deviated too much from Hegel’s initial theory. While I think the theme of reflexivity is very prominent in the reading and I was able to identify how this was portrayed, I fear it might be hard for me to dig even deeper into this concept, given it is so “out there” already. Perhaps I just need to use this lens on even more passages from the reading, or find a way to interpret it in a new way. Wittgenstein would probably see consciousness as the main differentia within the species. All consciousness is different, or differently perceived - evidenced by the different interpretations and definitions of consciousness there have been. Consciousness itself would be a result of interactions and inward reflection, not a single “thing”. This would in turn discredit the idea that the mind is a centralized system in charge of all our actions and thoughts. Freedom, according to Wittgenstein, might similarly be a network of small “liberties”. Social freedom, economic freedom, freedom from labor or bondage. They’re all conceptually different, but to be truly free would mean to be untied to any constraint. The definition of freedom, would therefore be a result of our actions, circumstances, and mindset. It can be individual, institutional, momentary, chronic…etc. The nature of definitions of freedom varies contextually, yet all these definitions will be valid. Wittgenstein would consider a self the amalgamation of everything that we define as self-consciousness. That would include our awareness of projection onto others, our values and thoughts from religion, our interactions with others. There is little commonality among any of these definitions from our readings, but the union of all of these strengthen the idea that all of these represent the “self”. Some of the themes we’ve explored over the past few weeks, both in Hegel’s writing and in general discussion can be tied to the show Westworld as a whole. As mentioned in class, the camera angles and set design are very intentional in portraying themes of entrapment, hierarchy, relationships…etc. A specific example that comes to mind is the costume design in Westworld. The sterile/fitted-looking clothing of the engineers and corporate people vs the old-timey, western appearance of the hosts. Beyond the literal meaning of this contrast, the visual contrast also separates the “self-conscious” from the unaware. I’ve given agency to clocks and watches. I give them a certain responsibility to “work” at all times. I still use it. Discussion about Eve being a rational figure. Often portrayed as the weaker individual who succumbs to Satan’s tricks. Yet, Milton portrays her as an incredibly rational person who deliberates and considers consequences. Despite her making the wrong decision, the feeling that ensues is not regret (which is a passive reaction), but acceptance and recalibration based on the new circumstances. This redefined the term “freedom” for me. Since the start of college I have been in a constant battle with myself. I cannot figure out how to balance academic stress with the desire to live happily in a new environment. My “master-self” (the self that wants me to work tirelessly to achieve good grades, transfer to Ross, get an internship…etc) is controlling my “slave-self” (the one actually doing everything) to a point where I’m starting to feel burnout (the upheaval of the slave). I find solace, however, in the fact that this will potentially lead to a future sense of relief. I think coming to a new country changed the way I interact and view myself. I learned to practice my language (not in fluency but in social interactions), my mannerisms, my tone and volume. The friendships I have now are so dissimilar to ones I’ve had in the past and it is because I am a different “self” from my past “self”. I had always thought of myself as an outgoing, carefree person. However now, inspecting my priorities, my habits and routines, that has changed, and so has my view of myself.
